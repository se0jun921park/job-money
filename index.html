Methodology & sources

How the numbers are built

  • Everyone starts college at 18 and retires at 65. Figures are in constant 2025 dollars, with no inflation or raises beyond the career's own pay curve.
  • Education cost is published tuition and required fees for every year of school. Living costs are left out, because people pay those in every career.
  • Pay rises from the starting salary to the peak salary over the listed years, then stays flat. Doctors earn AAMC resident stipends during residency.
  • Loans are federal Direct Unsubsidized loans at 2025-26 rates: 6.39% for undergraduate years and 7.94% for graduate years. Interest builds through school and residency, and repayment starts at the first full-salary job.
  • Net lifetime earnings are total pay from 18 to 65, minus tuition paid in cash and every loan payment, including interest.
  • Net earnings to date on the chart also subtracts the loan balance still owed, so the line starts below zero while debt is outstanding.
  • After tax applies 2025 federal brackets for a single filer, the standard deduction, Social Security and Medicare, and a flat 5% state tax.
  • Real outcomes vary widely by specialty, firm, city and school. Treat each line as a typical path, not a forecast. New federal borrowing caps for professional students begin in July 2026 and are not modeled here.
